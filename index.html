Assays

Three measurements that decide whether a design is worth keeping

Each assay is a fixed protocol. You choose which to run per batch, and every result carries its raw data.

Binding

Binding kinetics and affinity

Label-free biosensor measurements (bio-layer interferometry or surface plasmon resonance). The target is immobilized, your protein flows over it, and the instrument records how fast it attaches and how fast it lets go.

Reports
KD, kon, koff
Format
concentration series, 3 replicates
Relation
KD = koff / kon
Expression

Expression yield and quality

Each design is produced in a cell-free reaction and quantified. A design that cannot be made, or comes out at the wrong size, is the most common reason a binder fails, so we report it instead of hiding it.

Reports
yield (µg/mL), size check
Flags
no expression, truncation, low yield
Use
filters designs before deeper work
Thermostability

Melting temperature

Temperature is ramped while a fluorescence signal tracks unfolding (differential scanning fluorimetry). The midpoint of the transition is the melting temperature, Tm. Higher means the fold holds up under heat and storage.

Reports
Tm, onset, curve shape
Range
25 to 95 °C ramp
Use
ranks developability
Cell-free expression

Proteins made without growing cells

A cell-free reaction contains only the machinery needed to read DNA and build a protein: ribosomes, enzymes, amino acids and an energy source. Add your DNA and protein appears in hours, with no culture step.

  • Hours instead of days, since nothing has to grow or be harvested
  • Small volumes in plates, so thousands of designs fit one run
  • Tolerates proteins that would be toxic to a living host
  • Known limits: complex glycosylation is not reproduced, and some disulfide-rich proteins need adjusted conditions
Plate design

Controls on every plate, replicates on every design

Numbers from different plates are only comparable if the plates carry their own references. Ours do.

  • Blanks to set the baseline signal of the buffer alone
  • A positive control with a known binder, to confirm the assay is working that day
  • A negative control that should not bind, to measure background
  • Each design in triplicate, with spread reported next to the average
  • Plate-level QC flags a run before its data is delivered
